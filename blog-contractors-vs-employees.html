1099 Contractors vs. W-2 Employees: What Business Owners Should Know

The difference is more than which tax form you send. Understand how worker classification affects control, payroll, taxes, and day-to-day responsibilities.

What do “1099” and “W-2” actually mean?

A W-2 is the wage and tax statement an employer gives an employee. A 1099-NEC is generally used to report certain payments made to someone who is not an employee. The tax form reflects a working relationship, but it does not decide the worker’s legal classification by itself.

That distinction matters to every business hiring extra help, whether for marketing, maintenance, administration, or ongoing operations.

How the two arrangements compare

ConsiderationIndependent contractor (1099)Employee (W-2)
Working relationshipTypically offers services as an independent business or professional.Works as part of the employer’s business.
Direction of workGenerally has greater independence over how services are performed, consistent with applicable law.Employer generally has the right to direct how work is performed.
Tax reportingBusiness generally issues Form 1099-NEC when reporting rules apply.Employer issues Form W-2 reporting wages and applicable withholding.
Taxes and payrollIndependent contractors generally handle their own income and self-employment taxes; exceptions can apply.Employer generally handles wage withholding and required employer payroll taxes.
Benefits and protectionsNot ordinarily included in the hiring business’s employee benefit plans.Employee protections and benefits may apply depending on the law, employer, and role.

Potential advantages and tradeoffs of contractors

Genuinely independent contractors can make sense when a company needs a defined project, specialized expertise, or an outside business that serves multiple clients. The arrangement may offer flexibility, but rates can be higher, availability may be limited, and the hiring business cannot simply supervise an independent contractor as if they were an employee.

Potential advantages and tradeoffs of employees

Employees may make more sense for recurring duties, established shifts, ongoing training, and work that requires close day-to-day direction. An employer has more administrative obligations, including payroll and relevant wage, hour, leave, insurance, and workplace rules.

California businesses should be especially careful

Calling someone a “1099 contractor,” paying by invoice, or signing a contractor agreement does not automatically make the person an independent contractor. California generally applies the ABC test to many working relationships, with exceptions and other tests that may apply to particular circumstances. Federal agencies also assess how the work is actually performed.

Before deciding how to hire, define the responsibilities, the degree of independence, who supplies tools, how the work is managed, and whether the role is part of your usual business. If the classification is uncertain, ask a qualified employment or tax professional.

The takeaway

Choose the lawful working relationship first, then use the appropriate forms and payment process. A 1099 is not a shortcut around employee responsibilities, and a W-2 is not necessary for every truly independent service provider.

Keep facility-cleaning responsibilities clear

The same focus on clear responsibilities helps when arranging workplace cleaning. Whether upkeep is handled by employees or a separately engaged cleaning company, define the rooms, task frequency, supplies and access rules in writing. Worker classification and service contracts should be evaluated on their own terms.
