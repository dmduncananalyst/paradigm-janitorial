What is included vs billed separately?

Included services are the tasks and areas listed in the apartment cleaning agreement, while work outside that scope may require separate authorization. Clarify recurring cleaning, consumable supplies and specialty projects before service begins so nobody assumes an additional task is already covered.

Make the recurring scope specific

Shared area cleaning can include different responsibilities at different properties. Name the spaces, tasks and frequency rather than rely on a broad all cleaning phrase. Refilling a dispenser and supplying the contents are also separate commitments. A useful agreement explains these boundaries without needing to publish rates or an internal estimating method.

Identify additional service needs early

A unit turnover, post renovation cleanup or detailed floor project may fall outside the recurring shared area program. Ask the provider to confirm whether the work is already included or needs a separate arrangement. Do not assume a cleaner can add a large project simply because the crew is already on the property.

Agree before expanding the assignment

Identify who may approve a scope change and how it will be documented. Residents or tenants may request tasks the property has not authorized. The provider should have a clear contact for these requests rather than accept conflicting instructions. If a recurring need develops, review the agreement so the service remains understandable over time.

Review added work before it becomes an assumption

A recurring request can gradually change what occupants expect from a shared area program. If it was never included, discuss whether the agreement should be revised. This keeps service clear without presenting additional tasks as automatic obligations simply because a crew is already present.

The property should identify who may approve changes and how tenants or residents submit requests. A cleaner should not have to decide which person's instruction overrides the agreement. Written clarification of scope and authorization is useful even when the website publishes no prices or estimating details.

Consider residents and shared spaces together

Apartment cleaning takes place in a community where residents continue to use entrances, corridors and shared facilities. Explain access rules and which belongings must remain untouched. A cleaning visit should not become an assumption that personal property can be discarded or occupied units can be entered without authorization. Management remains responsible for property rules and maintenance decisions outside the cleaning agreement. When a shared space changes or a new amenity opens, review the materials and duties before adding it to the program. This keeps service useful to the community while making the boundary between cleaning, management and repairs understandable to everyone involved.

A practical example

A lobby cleaning program may cover its floors and accessible surfaces while a vacant unit turnover is arranged separately. The useful distinction is the agreed work and authorization, not a public price list.
