Social Security, What It Is and How It Works

An introduction to Social Security contributions, retirement benefits, and planning ahead.

What Social Security does

Social Security provides benefits to eligible retired workers, people with disabilities, and certain family members and survivors. It is not an individual savings account. Benefits depend on eligibility rules and a person’s covered earnings history.

How people pay into it

For most employees, Social Security payroll taxes are withheld from wages and employers contribute as well. Self employed workers generally pay through self employment taxes. The applicable tax rules and wage limits can change, so review current IRS guidance.

Why contributions during working years matter

Covered work builds the earnings record used to determine eligibility and benefit amounts. Working and contributing earlier may help someone build enough credits over time, although benefits are not determined by credits alone.

When benefits begin

Retirement benefits can generally begin at age 62 for eligible workers, but starting before full retirement age reduces monthly payments. Waiting beyond full retirement age can increase the monthly amount up to age 70. Individual decisions depend on health, work, finances, and family circumstances.

How to plan

Review your earnings history and personalized benefit estimates through your Social Security account. Compare claiming ages and consider other retirement savings alongside expected Social Security income.

Planning ahead for property upkeep

Long-term planning may include a family home, estate or building that someone must maintain. If you oversee a residential property in Paradigm’s service area, identify which shared rooms, entryways and other spaces need regular cleaning as occupancy changes.
