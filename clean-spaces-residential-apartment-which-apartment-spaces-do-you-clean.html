Which apartment spaces do you clean?

Apartment cleaning can cover agreed shared spaces and separately arranged unit interiors, subject to the provider's capabilities and property access. Identify the lobby, corridors, elevators, laundry rooms or other areas required so the service boundary is clear before work begins.

Separate common areas from private units

Access to an apartment community does not authorize entry to every resident's home. Common spaces and unit interiors should be identified separately, with appropriate permission for each. A property management agreement may cover the shared lobby without covering occupied apartments. Avoid letting the broad phrase apartment cleaning blur those boundaries.

Describe rooms with unusual needs

Fitness rooms, lounges and shared restrooms can contain equipment or surfaces requiring particular care. Outdoor areas can also need a different service from interior cleaning. Share material information and property restrictions before scheduling. The provider should confirm what it can maintain and which areas need a specialist or another trade.

Make the coverage understandable

A clear area list helps management review service and answer resident guides. It also identifies spaces the crew cannot access or is not authorized to enter. If the property adds a shared room or changes its use, request a scope review. New facilities should not silently become part of an agreement written for a different layout.

Keep authorization specific to the space

Permission to clean shared corridors is different from permission to enter an occupied unit or a resident's storage area. Identify which spaces are authorized and whether a separate appointment is required for unit interiors. A property service agreement should not blur access rights simply because the same cleaner works elsewhere in the building.

When a community adds a gym, lounge or other shared room, review the materials and equipment before adding it to the cleaning program. A new amenity may need different care or another qualified provider. The scope should reflect the actual facility rather than an outdated list.

Consider residents and shared spaces together

Apartment cleaning takes place in a community where residents continue to use entrances, corridors and shared facilities. Explain access rules and which belongings must remain untouched. A cleaning visit should not become an assumption that personal property can be discarded or occupied units can be entered without authorization. Management remains responsible for property rules and maintenance decisions outside the cleaning agreement. When a shared space changes or a new amenity opens, review the materials and duties before adding it to the program. This keeps service useful to the community while making the boundary between cleaning, management and repairs understandable to everyone involved.

A practical example

A program can include the lobby, shared corridors and laundry room while turnover units are arranged individually. That is a clear coverage arrangement when residents and management understand the separate responsibilities.
