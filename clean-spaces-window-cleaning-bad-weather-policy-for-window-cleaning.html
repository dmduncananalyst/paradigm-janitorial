Bad weather policy for window cleaning

Window cleaning may need to be postponed when weather makes access, equipment use or the finished result unsuitable. Ask the provider how weather decisions are communicated and how an interrupted or postponed visit will be rescheduled.

Separate a forecast from actual work conditions

A forecast alone does not describe the conditions at every side of a building. Wind exposure, access height and shelter can change what is practical on site. Rain may affect exterior work differently from interior glass. The decision should consider the proposed work and the conditions present rather than rely on an unconditional promise to work in all weather.

Understand the communication arrangement

Confirm who contacts the property if the visit changes and whether access appointments need to be moved. A storefront preparing for an early arrival has different coordination needs from an unoccupied office with authorized access. Ask what happens if weather becomes unsuitable after work begins. The agreement should make the rescheduling process understandable without inventing a weather guarantee.

Protect people and property first

Window cleaning can involve wet walking surfaces and access equipment. Unfavorable conditions should not encourage shortcuts to preserve a calendar appointment. The provider should explain which parts can proceed and which must wait. Avoid assuming that a lower floor or a short visit removes every weather concern. Safety and suitable access remain relevant at all heights.

Discuss interruption before it occurs

A property should know whether an unsuitable forecast triggers an advance call or whether the provider will assess conditions on arrival. The exact policy should come from the company rather than a generic article. Ask who handles a tenant access appointment if the visit moves.

Also clarify how completed and postponed portions are communicated. An interior area may have been serviced while an exterior area remains outstanding. That distinction helps management avoid assuming the entire job was canceled or the entire job finished, especially when several occupants coordinate access.

Apply the decision to the whole window area

Consider the glass together with the area around it. A door may have a film, a display may prevent interior access and a sill may hold personal belongings. Exterior panes can also have a different access requirement from the room inside. Tell the provider about these conditions so the proposed service is realistic. The property should know which components are included and which remain outside the scope. If a problem concerns a damaged seal, coating or piece of hardware, arrange an appropriate assessment rather than assume another glass wash will solve it. A clear result means understanding both what improved and what cleaning could not address.

A practical example

An exterior appointment may be postponed while accessible interior glass remains suitable for service. Whether a split visit makes sense depends on the agreed scope and property access, not a universal rule for every rainy day.
