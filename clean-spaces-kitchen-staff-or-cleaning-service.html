Should I have the kitchen staff clean the kitchen, or hire a cleaning service?

Kitchen staff should usually handle immediate food service cleanup that is part of their normal workflow, while a professional cleaning service can handle deeper facility cleaning, floors, detailed surfaces, trash, and scheduled sanitation tasks. The right split depends on whether the kitchen is a workplace breakroom, a commercial food operation, or something in between.

Decide what belongs to daily operations

Staff already working in the kitchen are usually closest to spills, dishes, food preparation surfaces, and equipment used during the shift. Those immediate tasks are often part of normal operations because waiting until the end of the day can create safety and workflow problems.

Separate detailed cleaning from food preparation

A professional cleaning service can take on work that is easier to schedule outside the active food period, such as floors, trash areas, walls, exterior surfaces of equipment, employee areas, and other facility cleaning. This keeps kitchen staff focused on their primary job while still giving the space a consistent reset.

Write down the handoff

Confusion happens when everyone assumes someone else is responsible. A simple scope can identify what kitchen staff completes before leaving and what the cleaning team completes during its visit. The handoff should be specific enough that there is no gray area around trash, floors, counters, sinks, or shared spaces.

Consider labor cost and consistency

Using higher paid kitchen staff for detailed janitorial work may not be the best use of their time. On the other hand, hiring a cleaning service for every small task may be unnecessary. The most practical setup often gives each group the work that fits its role and schedule best.
