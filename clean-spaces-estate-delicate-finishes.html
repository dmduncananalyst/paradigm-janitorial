Caring for Glass, Stone, and Delicate Estate Finishes

Identify each material and finish, collect its manufacturer or installer care instructions, and create an approved cleaning method for that surface before work begins. Confirm products and tools, test an inconspicuous area where appropriate, and stop for clarification when the material or existing damage is uncertain.

Make a surface record before choosing products

List the stone, wood, glass, metal, and coated surfaces in the cleaning scope. Record the material, finish, location, and any care documents supplied by the manufacturer or installer. Surfaces that look similar may need different methods. Include photographs of existing marks or damage so staff do not mistake them for dirt and keep working at the same spot with stronger products.

Match the method to the material

The Natural Stone Institute recommends neutral cleaners for routine stone care and warns against acidic cleaners on acid-sensitive stone such as marble and limestone. The National Wood Flooring Association recommends a cleaner intended for the floor’s finish. Use those principles alongside the specific care instructions for the installed material, and confirm any coatings or sealers. One general-purpose product should not become the default for every finish in the estate.

Make the approved approach easy for staff to follow

Keep a short surface-by-surface guide with the agreed products, dilution instructions, cloths, and tools. Store and identify supplies clearly so staff do not substitute a product because it happens to be nearby. Keep dirty tools away from delicate finishes, and agree which fixtures or objects may be moved. A supervisor should explain any changes when new materials are installed or the cleaning team changes.

Know when cleaning should stop

If a surface changes appearance, a mark does not respond to the approved method, or the finish is unknown, stop and report it. Repeated rubbing or switching to a stronger product can turn uncertainty into damage. Ask the installer or an appropriate restoration professional to assess work beyond routine cleaning. Keep any written instructions with the property’s surface records so the next person does not repeat the same experiment.

Care references: Natural Stone Institute, stone care · National Wood Flooring Association, maintenance
