Dodgers Playoff Baseball Has Los Angeles Buzzing Again

The Dodgers earned a first round bye, but October has already become a test of health, pitching, and execution as the Division Series shifts to Atlanta tied at one game apiece.

The Dodgers entered October with something every postseason team wants, a first round bye. Los Angeles finished as the National League's No. 2 seed, which meant the club skipped the Wild Card Series and went straight into the National League Division Series. That extra time mattered because several key players were either recently back from injury or still managing health issues.

The bye gave the Dodgers time they actually needed

Shohei Ohtani returned late in the regular season after dealing with right biceps and left knee issues. He is in the lineup as a hitter this postseason, but he is not expected to pitch. Freddie Freeman also dealt with right patellar tendinitis in September. Blake Treinen's season ended because of right shoulder inflammation, which leaves one fewer experienced option in a bullpen that already entered October with questions about how the final innings would be handled.

The Dodgers were still able to begin the Division Series close to full strength, but close to full strength is not the same as completely healthy. In October, that difference can matter quickly.

This round is already tight

Los Angeles opened the best of five Division Series against Atlanta with a 5 to 3 win at Dodger Stadium. Atlanta answered the next night with a 3 to 2 win, sending the series to Georgia tied at one game apiece.

That makes the next stretch important. Games 3 and 4 are scheduled in Atlanta, and if the series needs a deciding Game 5, it comes back to Los Angeles. There is very little room for a long slump in a five game series. One strong start, one missed opportunity with runners on base, or one bullpen decision can change the entire round.

What to watch next

The starting rotation is still one of the Dodgers' biggest strengths. Yoshinobu Yamamoto is lined up for Game 3 against Atlanta ace Chris Sale, which gives the next game the feel of a major pitching matchup. The Dodgers also need their offense to create more pressure after going 0 for 8 with runners in scoring position in Game 2.

The late innings are another storyline. Los Angeles has several pitchers who can finish games, but the team has not treated the ninth inning as one fixed role. That flexibility can be useful, but postseason games have a way of testing every bullpen decision.

There is a bigger goal behind this run

The Dodgers are not simply trying to get through one round. They entered the postseason as the back to back defending World Series champions, with a chance to become the first Major League team to win three straight titles since the Yankees from 1998 through 2000.

For now, though, the entire season has narrowed to Atlanta. The series is tied, both teams have already shown they can win close games, and the next few nights will decide whether Los Angeles gets another round of October baseball.
