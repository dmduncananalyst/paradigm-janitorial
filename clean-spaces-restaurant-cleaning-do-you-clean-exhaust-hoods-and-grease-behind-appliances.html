Do you clean exhaust hoods and grease behind appliances?

Exhaust hood and duct cleaning must be confirmed as a specific service rather than assumed to be part of ordinary restaurant cleaning. Grease behind appliances can be addressed only where the area is safely accessible and the agreed work does not require cleaners to disconnect or alter utility connections.

Separate the hood exterior from the exhaust system

Wiping an accessible hood exterior is not the same as cleaning the complete exhaust assembly. Filters, ducts, fans and connected systems have distinct access and service requirements. A proposal should identify the actual components included and the qualifications required for the work. Do not describe general kitchen cleaning as full exhaust system service without confirmation.

Check whether appliances can move safely

An appliance may appear movable while remaining connected to gas, electricity, water or drainage. Those connections can limit access behind it. Cleaning staff should not pull on a connected appliance merely to reach grease. Ask who is responsible for any necessary equipment handling and whether another qualified trade must prepare the area before cleaning can proceed.

Review accessibility before promising a result

Heavy buildup and difficult access can change the scope. The provider should assess the area rather than promise complete removal from every hidden surface. Identify which areas can be cleaned safely and which require another service. Cleaning visible grease should not be represented as a certification that an exhaust system or appliance is safe to operate.

Ask whether the proposed service covers the complete system

An exhaust system involves more than the visible stainless canopy. If the request concerns the system rather than accessible exterior surfaces, confirm the provider's actual specialist capability and any required documentation. Do not assume a restaurant cleaning advertisement covers every ventilation component.

For grease behind appliances, the access assessment is equally important. A connected unit may require authorized handling by another qualified person before cleaning is possible. The agreement should identify that responsibility. It should not encourage a cleaner to move utility connected equipment based on an assumption that hidden grease must be reached at any cost.

Keep the restaurant’s responsibilities clear

A restaurant cleaning project operates alongside the restaurant’s own food practices and equipment maintenance. Preparation areas, floors and equipment exteriors can have different requirements and different responsible people. Before the appointment, identify who prepares the space and who authorizes it to return to use. If an appliance or fixture needs repair, cleaning should not be represented as resolving that defect. Management also needs to understand what happens between professional visits, when the restaurant continues to operate. A clear cleaning agreement supports ongoing sanitation without claiming that a clean looking kitchen is proof of every food safety requirement or a guaranteed inspection outcome.

A practical example

A stainless hood canopy can look clean while grease remains inside ductwork that was never included in the agreement. The restaurant needs a clear distinction between accessible surface cleaning and a specialist exhaust system assignment.
